# Part A: Portugal and Brazil

As of: 2026-11-10

Scope: how records are accessed, whether automated reading (OCR / AI / scraping / bulk download) of page images is allowed, whether commercial use is allowed, and how to ask for a partnership or licence. Covers (1) Digitarq / DGLAB archives, (2) Portuguese civil registry (IRN) and the nationality-by-descent regime, (3) Brazilian civil registry (registrocivil.org.br / ON-RCPN).

Method and confidence labels used below:
- **Read**: I opened the page or document and read the text (browser text, or raw HTML/PDF text extracted locally). Quotes are verbatim.
- **Summary-tool read**: the page was fetched with a summarising fetch tool that returns a model-written summary, not the raw page. Short quotes in these cases are as returned by that tool and are marked "(summary tool)". Treat them as likely but not byte-verified.
- **Secondary**: law-firm or blog page. Used only as a lead, labelled as such.
- **UNVERIFIED**: appeared only in a search snippet, or I could not open the primary page.

No AI-specific, OCR-specific or scraping-specific term was found in ANY of the sources below. Where I say "not addressed", that means silence in the pages I could read, not permission.

## A. 0. Headline findings

1. **Digitarq publishes no machine-readable terms of use that I could open.** The only licence statement I could read is the help page: "Ao visualizar as imagens o utilizador será sempre informado da licença ao abrigo da qual as mesmas podem ser utilizadas." (When viewing the images the user is always told the licence under which they may be used.) The licence text itself is shown per image in the viewer, and I could not reach a viewer page (Digitarq search returned only "A atualizar dados ..." in my test on 2026-11-10). A search snippet claimed CC BY-SA 4.0; that is UNVERIFIED (the page it came from, `/oservices`, now returns 404).
2. **The binding DGLAB text is the 2015 reproduction regulation (Despacho n.º 6852/2015).** It makes commercial use and any "reutilização" not expressly provided for subject to specific DGLAB authorisation (art. 4) and forbids any use beyond the one authorised (art. 1.º n.º 19). For a paid service that reads images with AI, assume a written licence or protocol from DGLAB is needed unless the per-image licence in the viewer says otherwise.
3. **Portuguese law favours reuse in principle.** Lei 26/2016 art. 19(1): documents to which access is authorised "may be re-used for commercial or non-commercial purposes unless otherwise provided". Archives are inside the re-use regime (art. 20(g) excludes cultural institutions "except libraries ... museums, and archives"). Fees are allowed and, for archives, may include preservation costs and a return on investment (art. 23-A(3)). This gives a legal argument for a reuse request, but DGLAB's practice (case-by-case authorisation) is the operative rule.
4. **The Portuguese nationality law changed on 2026-05-19** (Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026). Grandchildren of Portuguese-born-by-origin nationals can still claim nationality of origin (art. 1(1)(d)), but now must also meet the Portuguese language/culture, rights-and-duties, loyalty, and criminal-record requirements of art. 6(1)(c)-(h). Pending procedures stay under the old law. The Government had 90 days to amend the Regulamento da Nacionalidade; a law-firm article says it had not by August 2026. I could not confirm the current status in the official gazette.
5. **Brazil's civil registry portal expressly prohibits commercial use and resale/intermediation with a mark-up** (registrocivil.org.br Termos de uso, point 5.3), restricts use of the data to the purpose of the request (2.1, 5.4), and has disabled creation of new company (CNPJ) accounts (5.6). Any evidence-pack business that orders certificates for customers should have each customer order in their own name, or negotiate an integration with ON-RCPN.
6. **DGLAB's own websites were taken offline on or about 2026-10-26** after a PHP security vulnerability (notice on dglab.gov.pt, read 2026-11-10). Digitarq itself is up, but the DGLAB regulations, contact pages and several district-archive sites return 404. Expect gaps in what can be re-verified until they are restored ("Previsão de reposição do serviço: a indicar").

## A. 1. Digitarq / Arquivo Nacional da Torre do Tombo / district archives (DGLAB network)

### Coverage
- Digitarq homepage (read): "Pesquise mais de 8 milhões de conteúdos dos arquivos da DGLAB associados a mais de 63 milhões de imagens" (search more than 8 million archive records linked to more than 63 million images). Source: https://digitarq.arquivos.pt/
- Digitarq was replaced by a new version in April 2025 (search snippet from district-archive notice pages, UNVERIFIED; I did not open those notices). The help page (read) lists tutorial videos including "Pesquisar registo de batismo (Arquivo Distrital do Porto)" and "Pesquisar livros digitalizados nos fundos paroquiais (Arquivo Distrital do Porto)", which confirms parish baptism/death series are searched through Digitarq. Source: https://digitarq.arquivos.pt/help and https://digitarq.arquivos.pt/en/help
- The DGLAB homepage (read) lists the DGLAB network as the Torre do Tombo (ANTT), the Arquivo Histórico Ultramarino, and district archives with their own sub-sites (Aveiro, Beja, Bragança, Castelo Branco, Évora, Faro, Guarda, Leiria, Portalegre, Santarém, Setúbal, Viana do Castelo, Vila Real, Viseu appear in the e-mail list; Porto is listed as an archive in the job notices). Braga and Coimbra district archives do not appear in that e-mail list. I did not verify who runs them (they may be outside the DGLAB network); UNVERIFIED. Source: https://dglab.gov.pt/
- What is actually digitised, by parish and year, varies. I could not browse the catalogue (search stalled), so per-district coverage is UNVERIFIED here. Search snippets indicate Porto and Braga parish registers are widely online and also catalogued by FamilySearch (see the FamilySearch note in the main file; not opened by me).
- Access rules for the underlying records:
  - Parish registers (pre-1911) are far older than any privacy threshold. The general archive rule (Decreto-Lei 16/93 art. 17 as rewritten by Lei 26/2016 art. 44; read in the Parliament's English text) is: documents with named data are accessible "30 years after the date of death of the persons to whom the documents relate; or ... where the date of death is not known, 40 years after the date of the documents, but not before 10 years have elapsed since the death is known". Source: https://www.parlamento.pt/sites/EN/Parliament/Documents/Lei26_2016.en.pdf
  - Civil registers: transitional rule in DL 324/2007 art. 15 (pgdlisboa text, read): books are transferred to the national archives entity once, from the date of the last entry, "Mais de 30 anos, quanto aos livros de assentos de óbito; ... Mais de 50 anos, quanto aos livros de assentos de casamento; ... Mais de 100 anos, quanto aos restantes livros de assentos" (more than 30 years for death books, 50 for marriage, 100 for the rest, which includes births). So old civil births (after 1911) become archive material after 100 years; younger ones stay with the conservatórias. Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=962&tabela=leis&so_miolo=S . Caveat: this is the 2007 text as consolidated on pgdlisboa; I did not check whether later amendments changed the thresholds.

### How records are accessed
- Free search and viewing at https://digitarq.arquivos.pt/. Help page (read): export of search results to Excel is for registered users only. The English page says "Only the first 500 results will be exported"; the Portuguese page says "Serão exportados apenas os primeiros 5000 resultados" (the two language versions disagree: 500 vs 5000). Registration gives a "my list" of records.
- Images are viewed by clicking the thumbnail in the document detail. The help page does not describe a download button, an API, or bulk export of images. I found no documented API (the Next.js front end calls internal `/_next/data/...` and `/api/auth/login` endpoints; these are not a public API and I did not test them).
- Paid reproductions (formal channel): Despacho 6852/2015 art. 8.º n.º 1 (read on Diário da República): "A formalização do(s) pedido(s) de reprodução pode ser presencial ou remota devendo ser efetuada através do preenchimento de formulário próprio, disponível no site da DGLAB ou através do sistema CRAV." Art. 5.2(e): "As reproduções digitais podem ser disponibilizadas ao cliente através de descarregamento pela Internet ou descarregadas através de conta ftp (File Transfer Protocol), ou outra forma de entrega eletrónica, uma vez verificado o pagamento integral do serviço prestado." Art. 1.º n.º 17: requests are priced "de acordo com tabela de preços em vigor". Source: https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/despacho/6852-2015-67540199 (publication: Diário da República n.º 118/2015, Série II, 2015-06-19).
- Civil certificates for post-1911 births: see section 2. Pre-1911 baptism entries (assentos paroquiais) are in the archives, requested from the archive that holds the book (secondary source: https://www.cidadaniaevisto.com.br/blog/certidao-nascimento-portugal-pedir-brasil, summary tool, flagged as promoting its own services).

### Automated reading / AI / bulk use
- **Explicit prohibition or permission for OCR, AI, scraping or bulk download: not found.** The Digitarq help page, the privacy policy (https://digitarq.arquivos.pt/privacyPolicy, summary tool: it "does not state a licence for reusing the content") and the DGLAB home page contain none.
- The binding rules are in the 2015 regulation (read). Verbatim:
  - Art. 1.º n.º 19: "O requerente compromete-se a não fazer qualquer outra utilização das imagens cedidas, senão aquela para a qual recebeu autorização expressa." (The requester commits to make no use of the images beyond the one for which express authorisation was given.)
  - Art. 1.º n.º 20: "A utilização diferente da prevista, salvo se antecedida de autorização expressa e inequívoca, será sancionada nos termos da lei." (Use different from that foreseen, unless preceded by express and unequivocal authorisation, is penalised by law.)
  - Art. 1.º n.º 7: "A matriz fotográfica, micrográfica ou digital, é propriedade da entidade detentora dos documentos reproduzidos" (the digital master belongs to the holding archive).
  - Art. 2.º n.º 1.2 (web publication): images for web use are supplied as thumbnails "não superior a 314 x 235 pixels, com marca ótica da entidade detentora" (not larger than 314 x 235 pixels, with the holder's optical mark) and the authorisation lasts 3 years. This is clearly aimed at publication, not model ingestion, but it shows DGLAB's default posture.
  - Art. 5.º n.º 4: "A execução dos serviços que revistam natureza de um projeto serão objeto de protocolo ou outro instrumento que se encontre adequado, a estabelecer com a entidade detentora dos documentos." (Services of the nature of a project are handled by protocol or other suitable instrument with the holding entity.) This is the clause a bulk or project-scale request would fall under.
  - Art. 1.º n.º 10 and n.º 11: requests for more than 70 % of a document or series must be reproduced in full, digitally, and the requester bears the cost of the work.
- The regulation applies to "todos os arquivos dependentes da DGLAB, de âmbito nacional e regional", with some points left to each archive's discretion (preamble).
- Lei 26/2016 (Parliament's English text, read; this is an unofficial translation of the law as amended by Leis 58/2019, 33/2020 and 68/2021). Art. 19(1): "Administrative documents to which access is authorised under this Law may be re-used for commercial or non-commercial purposes unless otherwise provided for in this Law or specific legislation." Art. 19(5): re-users "may not change the information contained therein, nor may they allow its meaning to be distorted, and must always mention the sources and the date on which the information was last updated". Art. 20 excludes from re-use, among others, "(b) documents whose intellectual property rights are held by third parties" and "(c) named documents, unless authorised by the data subject, there is a legal provision expressly providing for it, a legal basis under the applicable legislation on personal data for its processing or where the personal data can be anonymised". Art. 20(g) excludes "documents held by cultural institutions, except libraries, including libraries of higher education establishments, museums, and archives" (so archives stay inside the regime). Source: https://www.parlamento.pt/sites/EN/Parliament/Documents/Lei26_2016.en.pdf . The Portuguese consolidated text on diariodarepublica.pt could not be rendered by my fetch tool; the official Portuguese text should be checked before relying on article numbers.
- Open question that matters for AI: art. 20(c) (named documents) plus RGPD. Old parish records name people long dead, but any derived dataset linking them to living descendants is personal data of the descendants.

### Commercial use
- Verbatim from Despacho 6852/2015 (read):
  - Art. 2.º n.º 1.1(b): "As imagens solicitadas para fins comerciais devem ser objeto de autorização da DGLAB;"
  - Art. 2.º n.º 1.1(e): "As imagens não poderão ser vendidas nem trocadas com outras organizações ou pessoas, salvo se obtida por escrito a devida autorização da DGLAB" (images may not be sold or exchanged with others without written DGLAB authorisation).
  - Art. 2.º n.º 1.1(g): "A autorização é concedida para uma única utilização." (authorisation is for a single use)
  - Art. 2.º n.º 1.1(c) and art. 3.º: image credits are mandatory, including the optical mark of the holder, the document title, shelfmark, reference code, and "Imagem cedida pelo ANTT" and/or the holding entity.
  - Art. 4.º: "Outras utilizações, nomeadamente para fins comercias e/ou reutilização não previstas neste Regulamento ficam sujeitas a autorização específica para o efeito por parte da DGLAB." (Other uses, namely commercial and/or re-use not provided for here, require specific DGLAB authorisation.)
- Fees: Lei 26/2016 art. 23-A(3) (English text, read): where the requested documents are held by an archive, fees "shall also include the costs of its collection, production, preservation as well as storage and acquisition of rights, and may be increased by a reasonable return on investment". Art. 23(3)(a): re-use is free of charge for "documents made available via the Internet under Articles 10 and 11" (this could be argued to cover images placed online, but whether DGLAB treats Digitarq images that way is not stated anywhere I could read). Art. 23(3)(b): free "for educational or research and development purposes".
- Note: the Despacho is from 2015 and predates the 2021 amendments to Lei 26/2016 and the April 2025 Digitarq relaunch. I did not find a replacement regulation, but could not search the DGLAB site (down).

## A. 2. Portuguese civil registry online (IRN) and nationality by descent

### Coverage
- Civil registry (post-1911). Civil registration became mandatory in 1911. Older (pre-1911) entries are parish registers held in archives (see section 1). Secondary source only (https://www.cidadaniaevisto.com.br/blog/certidao-nascimento-portugal-pedir-brasil, summary tool): "assentos anteriores a 1911, ou com mais de 100 anos, costumam estar nos arquivos distritais ou no Torre do Tombo, e não aparecem no Civil Online". The 100-year link to the archives is consistent with DL 324/2007 art. 15 (section 1), but the claim that the online service omits them is UNVERIFIED against an official page.

### How certificates are requested; who may request; price; turnaround; formats
- Official service page (summary tool), https://justica.gov.pt/Servicos/Pedir-certidao-de-nascimento:
  - Who: "Qualquer pessoa pode pedir uma certidão de nascimento sua ou de terceiro." Exceptions mentioned: adopted persons' records and records of a person who changed sex and first name.
  - Online ("Civil Online"): with Cartão de Cidadão certificate, a professional card (lawyers, notaries, solicitors), or without authentication; pay by Multibanco or credit card.
  - In person or by post: conservatória do registo civil, Loja de Cidadão, IRN "Espaço dos Registos", or a Portuguese consulate abroad; by post to any conservatória.
  - Price: online certificate EUR 10; paper EUR 10 for Segurança Social / abono de família purposes; paper EUR 20 for other purposes.
  - Validity: the online certificate can be consulted with an access code for six months; "Entregar o código da certidão online é o mesmo que entregar uma certidão de nascimento em papel."
  - Turnaround: not stated. Paper certificates in the international model must be requested in person. The page did not mention a "certidão permanente" or a "certidão integral" by name.
- Legal basis for third-party requests (read): Código do Registo Civil art. 214.º n.º 1, text as shown in DL 324/2007 on pgdlisboa: "Qualquer pessoa tem legitimidade para requerer certidão dos registos, salvo as excepções previstas nos números seguintes." Exceptions: adoption proceedings (n.º 3) and secret "perfilhação" records (n.º 4). Art. 215.º n.º 4: "As certidões são emitidas imediatamente após a recepção da requisição" and n.º 3 allows electronic certificates with the same evidential value. I did not check whether the current code has later amended these paragraphs. Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=962&tabela=leis&so_miolo=S
- Practical use by Brazilians (secondary, summary tool): Civil Online can be used from Brazil without authentication; certificates for the citizenship file must be legalised (Portuguese certificates used in Brazil need an apostille issued in Portugal; Brazilian certificates used in Portugal need an apostille). The nationality guide says foreign certificates are legalised "por apostila ou visto do consulado português" (search-result summary; the primary guide page I opened lists "a sua certidão de nascimento legalizada" only).
- Legal instrument creating the online certificate: Portaria 181/2017 (found in search only, could not open the text; UNVERIFIED).

### Nationality by descent: current state (verified against primary texts)
- **Law in force:** Lei n.º 37/81 (Lei da Nacionalidade) as amended by Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, de 18 de maio (Diário da República n.º 95, 1.ª série, 2026-05-18). The official PDF (hosted by the Madeira regional government; I read arts. 1 to 8 of the amending law and the republished text) shows: "Aprovada em 1 de abril de 2026", "Promulgada em 3 de maio de 2026". Art. 8.º: "A presente lei entra em vigor no dia seguinte ao da sua publicação." That is 2026-05-19. Source: https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_Organica_1_2026.pdf
- **Transitional rule** (art. 7.º): "Aos procedimentos administrativos pendentes à data da entrada em vigor da presente lei aplica-se a Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro, na redação anterior à presente lei." (Procedures pending on 2026-05-19 stay under the old text.)
- **Regulation deadline** (art. 4.º): "O Governo procede às necessárias alterações ao Regulamento da Nacionalidade Portuguesa ... no prazo de 90 dias a contar da publicação da presente lei." (About 2026-08-16.) Status after that date: a law-firm article (https://gsnadv.com/en/2026/09/03/nacionalidade-portuguesa-para-netos-o-que-muda-em-2026/, summary tool) says the regulation "has not yet been adapted to Organic Law No. 1/2026" and the deadline had passed. Another blog (https://www.cidadaniaevisto.com.br/blog/nova-lei-nacionalidade-portuguesa-2026, summary tool) says the test format was still pending as of September 2026. I did not find the Diário da República for any amended regulation; current status as of 2026-11-10 is UNVERIFIED.
- **Consolidated text of arts. 1 and 6** (read, pgdlisboa 13th version, "Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, de 18/05"): https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=614&tabela=leis&ficha=1&pagina=1&so_miolo=S
  - Art. 1.º n.º 1 alínea c): children of a Portuguese mother or father born abroad are Portuguese by origin "se tiverem o seu nascimento inscrito no registo civil português ou se declararem que querem ser portugueses".
  - Art. 1.º n.º 1 alínea d): "Os indivíduos com, pelo menos, um ascendente de nacionalidade portuguesa originária do 2.º grau na linha reta que não tenha perdido essa nacionalidade, se declararem que querem ser portugueses e possuírem laços de efetiva ligação à comunidade nacional" (individuals with at least one second-degree direct-line ancestor Portuguese by origin who has not lost it, if they declare the wish and have effective ties to the national community). This is the grandchildren route.
  - Art. 1.º n.º 3 (new): "A atribuição da nacionalidade portuguesa ao abrigo da alínea d) do n.º 1 pressupõe o preenchimento dos requisitos constantes das alíneas c) a h) do n.º 1 do artigo 6.º" (that is: Portuguese language and culture test or certificate, knowledge of fundamental rights and duties and state organisation, solemn adherence to democratic principles, no conviction above 3 years for the listed crimes, no threat to national security, not subject to UN/EU restrictive measures).
  - Art. 6.º n.º 10: nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries are presumed to meet the language requirement "salvo nos casos em que a falta de domínio da língua portuguesa, evidenciada pelo requerente junto dos serviços competentes, seja manifesta".
  - Great-grandchildren: no attribution. Art. 6.º n.º 8: "O Governo pode conceder a nacionalidade, com dispensa do requisito previsto na alínea b) do n.º 1, aos indivíduos que sejam descendentes em 3.º grau na linha reta de portugueses originários e que tenham residência legal em território nacional há pelo menos cinco anos." (Discretionary naturalisation, waiving the 7/10-year residence requirement, for third-degree descendants with at least five years of legal residence in Portugal.)
  - Art. 21.º n.º 4: nationality of origin under alíneas c) and d) is proved "pelas menções constantes do assento de nascimento lavrado por inscrição no registo civil português ou pelo registo da declaração de que depende a atribuição".

Documents: the applicant's legalised birth certificate; the birth certificate of the parent who is the child of the Portuguese grandparent; the birth certificate of the Portuguese grandparent; criminal record certificates from the countries of birth, nationality, and residence since age 16, with certified translation if needed; the signed declaration. The page says the cost section exists but gave no figure in the text I received; call Linha Justiça 800 910 220.
- **Online filing platform (official page, summary tool):** https://justica.gov.pt/servicos/submeter-pedido-de-nacionalidade . "A submissão online de pedido de nacionalidade destina-se exclusivamente a profissionais (advogados e solicitadores)." Mandatory for lawyers and solicitors since 2023-12-01; portal nacionalidadeonline.justica.gov.pt. "O custo do pedido de nacionalidade portuguesa varia consoante a tipologia de pedido." Linha Registo: +351 211 950 500. The page says changes to the law "entram em vigor no dia 19 de maio".

### Automated reading / AI / bulk use
- Not addressed on justica.gov.pt or in the Código do Registo Civil text I read. Civil Online is a per-certificate, per-request service (price per certificate, access code valid six months). No bulk index or API was found. Treat certificate images and data as personal data (RGPD) once they concern persons who may be alive.
- Intermediaries: DECO PROteste warns about commercial intermediaries that charge several times the official price (search snippet, UNVERIFIED; not opened). # Part B: Italy and FamilySearch

As of: 2026-11-10

Scope: how records are accessed, whether automated reading (OCR / AI / scraping / bulk download) of page images is allowed, whether commercial use is allowed, and how to ask for a partnership or licence. Written for a planned paid service that uses AI to read page images of parish and civil records and builds citizenship-by-descent evidence packs (Brazil to Portugal/Italy).

Verification convention: a quote or fact is "verified" only if I opened the page and read it in this session (browser pane or WebFetch). WebFetch returns a model-written summary of a page, not raw text; where I relied only on that, I say "via WebFetch summary". Anything seen only in a search snippet is marked UNVERIFIED. This is research, not legal advice; the legal conclusions below should be checked by an Italian lawyer before launch.

## B. Headline findings

1. **FamilySearch is closed to the planned use.** Its Terms of Use bar commercial use, bulk download and scraping without prior written permission, and its developer rules say third-party apps do not get the historical-records collection and must not feed FamilySearch data into AI. Only a written permission or partner agreement could change this.
2. **Antenati's legal notes forbid for-profit use of the images**, restrict use to "strictly personal", and ban mirroring without an agreement with the Direzione generale Archivi. At the same time the site technically exposes public IIIF manifests and images and its robots.txt does not disallow crawling. The legal text, not the technical openness, is what governs. The route to a licence is the Direzione generale Archivi (and the individual Archivio di Stato for high-resolution copies, which are priced under the Ministry's tariff decrees).
3. **Antenati images have no legal value for citizenship.** The portal says so itself. Certified copies must come from the Archivio di Stato or the comune.
4. **The Italian citizenship-by-descent market is much narrower since 2025.** Art. 3-bis (DL 36/2025, converted by L. 74/2025) means most Brazil-born descendants without an Italian-only parent or grandparent are treated as never having acquired citizenship, unless they filed by 27 March 2025. The Constitutional Court upheld the rule in judgment 63/2026 (30 April 2026) and, in order 147/2026 (deposited 23 July 2026), referred a narrower EU-law question to the CJEU and suspended those proceedings.

### Automated reading / AI / bulk use

- Not addressed by name. The legal notes contain no explicit text on OCR, AI, scraping or bulk download. The Contatti page and notes say nothing on rate limits. What the notes do say (Note legali, https://antenati.cultura.gov.it/note-legali/):

Conclusion: automated bulk harvesting is not allowed without an agreement; per-user, on-demand viewing/processing is a legal grey area to be cleared in writing.

### Commercial use

- Note legali: "E' comunque esclusa ogni utilizzazione a fini di lucro delle immagini." Flat prohibition on for-profit use of the images as published on the portal.
- High-definition images are available from the holding Archivio di Stato "secondo quanto prescritto dai tariffari vigenti: D.M. 21 Marzo 2024, n. 108 recante modifiche al decreto del Ministro della cultura 11 aprile 2023, rep. n. 161 'Linee guida per la determinazione degli importi minimi dei canoni e dei corrispettivi per la concessione d'uso dei beni in consegna agli istituti e luoghi della cultura statali'." (Note legali.) So the current fee framework is DM 161/2023 as amended and its annex replaced by DM 108/2024 (21 March 2024).
- What I could verify about DM 161/2023 and DM 108/2024:
  - The DM 161/2023 text page (Edizioni Europee copy) says the rubric was replaced and the annex superseded by DM 108/2024; each institute adopts its own fee lists, which may be higher than the annex, and the annex applies where an institute has none (via WebFetch summary of https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/221/zn14_06_062.html). That page does not contain the fee schedule itself.
  - A trade-press summary says DM 108/2024 "va a sostituire completamente" the 2023 text; makes free, among others, reproductions "eseguite autonomamente da chiunque, a scopo non lucrativo e non destinate alla vendita", scientific and educational publications and open-access publications; keeps charges for other uses by format and purpose (web, print, very high resolution, 3D) with multipliers; and says "non è previsto alcun rimborso per le immagini già disponibili online e liberamente scaricabili" (via WebFetch summary of https://www.finestresullarte.info/attualita/riproduzione-immagini-beni-culturali-cambia-disciplina-aumentano-gratuita). Secondary source, not the decree.
  - UNVERIFIED: the actual euro amounts per image or per use, the exact legal definition of "scopo di lucro", and how the tariff treats a subscription AI service. The annex PDFs (e.g. on the Archivio di Stato di Trento and Pistoia sites) are only served as binary downloads here, and I had no PDF reader in this environment, so I could not read them. The decree must be read in the original before pricing anything.
- Interpretation: an AI service sold to paying families that ingests Antenati images falls squarely in the "fini di lucro" wording unless DG Archivi authorises it. Any plan that relies on Antenati images needs written authorisation or a licence.
- Legal value: "I documenti individuati ed eventualmente acquisiti nel Portale non hanno valore legale e non possono essere utilizzati per finalità di carattere giuridico e amministrativo, in particolare per le richieste di cittadinanza da parte dei discendenti degli emigrati italiani." (https://antenati.cultura.gov.it/il-portale/richieste-di-certificati/). Antenati is a discovery source only for citizenship packs.

## B. 2. Italian comuni (certificates) and citizenship law for descendants

### How certificate requests work (verified vs not)

- Legal basis: DPR 396/2000, art. 107, "Estratti per copia integrale": civil-status extracts "possono essere rilasciati dall'ufficiale dello stato civile per copia integrale soltanto quando ne è fatta espressa richiesta da chi vi ha interesse e il rilascio non è vietato dalla legge." The full copy must contain "la trascrizione esatta dell'atto", the marginal annotations, and a conformity attestation (verified at Normattiva: https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.del.presidente.della.repubblica:2000-11-03;396~art107).
- Who keeps what: Antenati says civil-status registers older than the second-copy deposit are in the State Archives, while "la documentazione anagrafica è conservata dai Comuni; per tale motivo, è necessario rivolgersi direttamente ad essi per richiedere informazioni e certificati" and that authentic copies of what is shown on Antenati come from the Archivio di Stato holding the original (https://antenati.cultura.gov.it/il-portale/richieste-di-certificati/ and FAQ 10). Authentic copies bear stamp duty "a meno che l'uso ... rientri tra quelli per i quali è prevista l'esenzione" (table B to DPR 642/1972); the requester must state the use.
- Extract per summary versus full copy: the full copy reproduces the entry with annotations; extracts are shorter. A consular form I could not read (binary PDF) is described in a search snippet as asking for an "estratto dell'atto di nascita ... in esenzione di tassa per uso cittadinanza" (UNVERIFIED). Consulate instructions (esteri.it, below) call for civil-status records of birth and marriage, legalised and officially translated where foreign.
- Third parties / agencies: a legal-news explainer (https://www.studiocataldi.it/articoli/46582-copia-integrale-atto-di-nascita-cos-e-e-come-richiederla-online.asp, via WebFetch summary) says a full copy can be requested by the person named, a legal representative, anyone with a reasoned legally relevant interest, or "un terzo delegato dall'intestatario" (an heir's delegation for a deceased person). That agrees with art. 107's "chi vi ha interesse" test, but each comune sets its own form and may require its own delega format. UNVERIFIED for any specific comune; I did not find a comune that publishes a rule about agencies. A comune may ask for a power of attorney or a signed delega plus ID copies.
- PEC and fees: UNVERIFIED. I found no verified comune page giving a PEC procedure, a fee per certificate for citizenship use, or response times. A search snippet mentions a comune charging 50 euro per full copy for acts from 1800-1970 (Pinerolo) but I could not open it (host unreachable), so not relied on. Expect fees and delays to differ by comune.
- Consulate role in the process: the consulate or the comune where the applicant lives (in Italy) takes the application; the consulate has authority to verify the descent file and to issue the "non rinuncia" certificate (esteri.it page below). Italian consulates do not obtain the Italian ancestors' records for you; applicants usually obtain them from the Italian comune or archive and bring them to the appointment.

### State of the citizenship-by-descent law (as of 2026-11-10)

All of this is from official or near-official texts I read, except where marked UNVERIFIED.

- **The reform.** Decree-Law 28 March 2025 n. 36, converted with amendments by Law 23 May 2025 n. 74, introduced art. 3-bis of Law 91/1992. It reads (as quoted in Constitutional Court judgment 63/2026): "è considerato non avere mai acquistato la cittadinanza italiana chi è nato all'estero anche prima della data di entrata in vigore del presente articolo ed è in possesso di altra cittadinanza, salvo che ricorra una delle seguenti condizioni":
  - a) status recognised, under the rules in force on 27 March 2025, on an application filed with the consulate or mayor by 23:59 Rome time that day;
  - a-bis) same, but with an appointment communicated by that day;
  - b) status established by a court on a claim filed by that deadline;
  - c) "un ascendente di primo o di secondo grado possiede, o possedeva al momento della morte, esclusivamente la cittadinanza italiana";
  - d) a parent or adoptive parent resided in Italy at least two continuous years after acquiring Italian citizenship and before the child's birth or adoption.
  Source: https://www.cortecostituzionale.it/scheda-pronuncia/2026/63 (full text read).
- **Meaning for descendants born in Brazil.** A descendant born in Brazil (who is a Brazilian citizen) qualifies only if filed by 27 March 2025 (a, a-bis, b), or if a parent or grandparent held only Italian citizenship (c), or if a parent lived in Italy two continuous years (d). That is the "two generations" effect: great-grandparent-based claims now fail unless the claim was filed by that date. This is my reading of the quoted text; the Ministry's own summary says the same in substance (https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-opportunita/italiani-all-estero/cittadinanza/, via WebFetch summary). Supporting documents named there for "exclusive Italian citizenship": negative citizenship certificates, certificates of renunciation of foreign citizenship, certificates of non-enrolment in foreign electoral registers; for residence, a historical residence certificate.
- **Constitutional Court judgment 63/2026** (Tribunale di Torino referral, hearing and decision 11 March 2026, deposited 30 April 2026, Gazzetta Ufficiale 6 May 2026 n. 18). The dispositivo declares not well-founded the questions under arts. 2, 3 Cost. and under art. 117(1) Cost. in relation to art. 9 TEU and art. 20 TFEU, and inadmissible the questions under art. 15(2) of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and art. 3(2) of Protocol 4 ECHR (all verified from the dispositivo at the URL above). In effect the rule survives the first challenge, including its application to people born before it came into force. The same text records the State's argument that about "60 milioni circa" descendants of emigrants were estimated.
- **Constitutional Court order 147/2026** (hearing and decision 9 June 2026, deposited 23 July 2026; referrals by the Tribunali of Mantova and Campobasso, joined). The Court itself referred to the CJEU under art. 267 TFEU: "se gli artt. 9 del Trattato sull'Unione europea e 20 del Trattato sul funzionamento dell'Unione europea ostino all'adozione di una disciplina quale quella prevista dall'art. 3-bis ... nella parte in cui configura una preclusione originaria all'acquisto della cittadinanza italiana a carico di chi è nato all'estero anche prima della data di entrata in vigore dello stesso art. 3-bis ed è in possesso di altra cittadinanza", and "sospende il presente giudizio sino alla definizione della suddetta questione pregiudiziale" (https://giurcost.org/decisioni/2026/0147s-26.html, full text read; the Corte's own page returned a CAPTCHA, which I did not bypass). A commentary notes a CJEU preliminary ruling usually takes over a year (https://lentepubblica.it/cittadini-e-imprese/riforma-cittadinanza-iure-sanguinis-la-consulta-interpella-la-corte-di-giustizia-ue/, via WebFetch summary). I found no CJEU case number and no CJEU ruling.
- **What this means in practice (as of 2026-11-10):** art. 3-bis remains in force; the Constitutional Court has not struck it down; one question (EU citizenship) is pending at the CJEU, so a reversal is possible in 2027-28 but cannot be planned on. Judgment 142/2025 and an Avellino order of 18 September 2026 appear in search results only (UNVERIFIED).
- **Administrative rules after the reform.** The Ministry page lists fees: EUR 600 for adult recognition applications (consular fee table), EUR 250 for acquisition/reacquisition declarations. The São Paulo consulate page says recognition is "subordinato alla prenotazione di un appuntamento tramite il portale Prenot@mi", that the applicant must attend in person ("Il richiedente dovrà presentarsi personalmente"), a fee of EUR 600 per adult paid in reais, and that applications are assessed "esclusivamente in conformità alla normativa vigente al momento della presentazione dell'istanza". Its eligibility list has three categories: parent exclusively Italian at the applicant's birth; second-degree ascendant exclusively Italian; parent with two continuous years' residence in Italy (https://conssanpaolo.esteri.it/it/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-straniero/cittadinanza/cittadinanza-iure-sanguinis/, via WebFetch summary). The consulate notice says applications received after 27 March 2025 "valutate alla luce della nuova normativa" and that people under the previous rules were already called (https://conssanpaolo.esteri.it/it/news/dal_consolato/2025/08/informazioni-cittadinanza/, via WebFetch summary). Delays and booking: none of the official pages states a wait time. A Brazilian news site (italianismo.com.br, Nov 2025) reports consular delays, with a senator quoted on "atrasos prolongados" and no figures; other claims (waits over 10 years, minors' calendar full to end of 2026) are search-snippet only and UNVERIFIED.
- **Impact on the product.** The addressable market for an Italy pack is now: (a) people who filed or had an appointment by 27 March 2025 and still need documents; (b) people with a parent or grandparent who held only Italian citizenship (rare: most Brazilian-born ancestors held Brazilian citizenship, so this is mostly Italian-born grandparents who never naturalised, and the grandparent line is where record evidence is needed); (c) minors under the declarations route; (d) pending court cases and the CJEU outcome. The Portuguese route (not covered here) is not affected by this law.

## B. 3. FamilySearch (familysearch.org)

### Coverage (relevant collections)

- Italy: many "Italy, [Province], Civil Registration (State Archive)" collections (the Italy civil-registration hub lists dozens, Agrigento to Viterbo) built from the same State-Archive filming that feeds Antenati. The wiki hub page says FamilySearch provides images and indexes "subject to contractual limitations and changes" (via WebFetch summary of the Italy, Civil Registration wiki page).
- Brazil: the Brazil Civil Registration wiki page lists FamilySearch collections including Rio Grande do Sul 1860-2006 (index and images), Santa Catarina 1850-1999 (images), Rio de Janeiro 1829-2012, São Paulo 1925-1995, Paraná 1852-1996, Espírito Santo 1850-2019, Pernambuco, Pará, Ceará and others; Rio Grande do Sul miscellaneous records 1748-1998 are images only (https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Brazil_Civil_Registration, read in browser). The Rio Grande do Sul civil registration page describes birth, marriage and death records and says some indexes were made with Computer Aided Indexing (https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Brazil,_Rio_Grande_do_Sul,_Civil_Registration_-_FamilySearch_Historical_Records).
- Portugal: "Portugal, Braga, Catholic Church Records, 1530-1911" (baptisms, marriages, deaths/burials from the Archdiocese of Braga, from the Arquivo Distrital de Braga; browsable by waypoints; contractual image-visibility notice) (https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Portugal,_Braga,_Catholic_Church_Records_-_FamilySearch_Historical_Records).

### Image restrictions on collections

- General help article: "On FamilySearch, some images have restrictions or limitations that determine who can see them or where you can see them. Guests without the necessary permissions see a restricted icon and a message such as 'Unable to Display Image Group.'" Images may be viewable only at a FamilySearch center, or a center or affiliate library, or on a third-party site (sometimes for a fee). "We don't have permission to share this image. Access is usually determined by the record's creator or custodian or by data privacy laws." (https://articles.familysearch.org/hc/en-us/articles/41693898891931-What-are-the-image-restrictions-in-Historical-Records). It also says a document icon with a star, "Search Full-Text Transcripts", "indicates that AI has been used to transcribe records in the collection".
- Italy-specific (Cuneo State Archive collection, 1795-1915, "Known Issues" page, read in browser): "Images for this collection are only available for viewing to members of the sponsoring organization (The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints), and at FamilySearch Centers"; "Italian Data Privacy rules prohibit viewing the following records: All birth images must be 100 years or older; all marriage images must be 70 years or older, and all death images must be 70 years or older"; folders containing any record inside the cut-off are restricted; "There is a no print/no save restriction with these collections at the request of the record custodians"; and a note that users can be redirected to "Il Portale Antenati" for browse-only images (https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Italy,_Cuneo,_Civil_Registration_(State_Archive)_-_FamilySearch_Historical_Records/Known_Issues). Other Italian provinces' Known Issues pages exist (search results) but I read only Cuneo.
- Brazil Rio Grande do Sul civil registration and Portugal Braga: the wiki pages carry the generic "Index and Image Visibility" notice only; I did not test individual images, so no per-collection restriction is confirmed. UNVERIFIED for each collection's actual image visibility; the Brazilian and Portuguese collections may well be open but must be tested while signed in.

### How records are accessed

- Web: search by name (index) or browse images by waypoint; account required for many views. Full-Text Search uses "AI and advanced handwriting recognition technology to create transcripts from handwritten historical documents" and "isn't yet available for all FamilySearch image collections" (FamilySearch blog, https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/ai-developments-genealogy). AI-indexed records are first read by AI "Volunteers follow behind the AI to check its work."
- API: free, "Access to the FamilySearch REST API and the various endpoints is free for both individuals and organizations" (https://developers.familysearch.org/ homepage, read in browser). The API gives Family Tree, Genealogies, Places, Memories (photos/stories) and Search and Match. It does not give third parties the historical-records images: "third-party applications do not have adequate access privileges to the FamilySearch historical records collection. This is primarily due to legal issues on what data third-party applications are allowed to show versus what FamilySearch is allowed to show." Record Hinting apps must send the user to FamilySearch to analyse and attach matches (https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/compatibility-checklist, read in browser).
- Access levels (via WebFetch summary of https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/getting-started): Beta key issued automatically ("an old snapshot of production data"); Production requires acceptance in the Compatible Solution Program and completing the compatibility process.

### Automated reading / AI / bulk use

- Site Terms of Use (updated 2023-09-01), https://www.familysearch.org/en/legal/terms (read in browser): "unless you have received our prior written permission, you will not use any software, technology, or device to perform a bulk download, to scrape content, or to otherwise harvest any information or materials from this site." Also: "You agree not to circumvent any of the controls or usage rules we have implemented." No explicit "OCR" or "AI" text on that page: an automated reader running on FamilySearch images would, in practice, be bulk downloading or harvesting, and many images are no-print/no-save by custodian request.
- Developer rules on AI (via WebFetch summaries): the AI Utilization Disclosure Addendum says the provider "must not use any of FSI's data, materials, confidential information, or other content of any kind" with AI, including as input or as training or fine-tuning data, "without FSI's express prior written permission. This Addendum does not constitute such permission." Providers must give FamilySearch notice before supplying content created with generative AI and must disclose AI use to end users (https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/ai-disclosure-addendum). The compatibility process page repeats that FamilySearch data "may never be used with AI, including as input or training data" and that apps using undocumented APIs are delisted and have keys disabled (https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/compatibility-review-process).
- Rate limits: "Requests to the FamilySearch API are rate-limited on a per-user basis", with HTTP 429 and a Retry-After header; limits are processing-time budgets (example: 18 seconds of execution per 1-minute window) (https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/throttling, via WebFetch summary). The throttling page says nothing about a bulk ban; the bulk ban is in the Terms of Use above.

### Commercial use

- Terms of Use: materials may be used "only for your personal, noncommercial use"; "You may not use this site or information found at this site ... to sell or promote products or services, to solicit clients, or for any other commercial purpose." Professional genealogists may research "for a client for the client's personal, noncommercial use" but "may not solicit or arrange business, promote themselves, or receive payments on this site or on our property." "Any other use ... including any use by organizations or legal entities, is not permitted without our prior written permission." (same page).
- API: free of charge and open to organisations, but approval criteria say a solution should "support FamilySearch's mission rather than compete", should not raise load, and solutions that only take data out of FamilySearch without value back to Family Tree may be rejected (https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/app-approval-considerations, via WebFetch summary; I did not see an explicit ban on charging users).
- Data rules for partners: deceased persons' data retrieved by authenticated users may be saved; living persons' data must stay private; cached data must be cleared at session end (compatibility review page).

### How to request partnership / permission

- API partnership: apply with the Third-Party Service Provider Application Form (linked from the developer homepage as https://forms.office.com/r/EstYaQAZSm and https://www.familysearch.org/innovate/apply, which redirects to the developer site); approval and keys come by email. Steps (via WebFetch summary of the review page): apply for Read Compatibility from "My App"; sign the Compatible Product Affiliate Agreement, Security Assessment and Production App Key Request and Use Agreement; FamilySearch evaluates and lists accepted apps in the Solutions Gallery. Only registered businesses or non-profits are eligible. No fees and no review durations are stated. Write-compatibility needs contact with Developer Support when nearly complete. The checklist requires, for all apps, "Apply to become a Solutions Provider", "Obtain acceptance in the Solutions Program", use of the Compatible logo, and use of the AI Utilization Disclosure Addendum when the app uses AI.
- Developer support: devsupport@familysearch.org (for key and callback issues) and the forum developers.familysearch.org/main/discuss/ (via WebFetch summary of https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/developer-support). That page lists no partnerships contact or response time.
- Permission outside the API (use of images or data beyond personal use): the Terms of Use give a "Request Permission" link and say "The approximate time frame for receiving a reply is 45 days. Requests involving considerable quantities of material may take longer." That form is the only documented route for the bulk/AI use the planned product would need.
- Record custodians' side: for Italian images, the permissions come from the DG Archivi/State Archives (the 2011 agreement), so FamilySearch cannot grant more than the custodian allows.

### Open questions

- Whether FamilySearch would grant written permission for AI reading of image collections for a commercial service, and whether it would accept the AI Utilization Disclosure Addendum for a product that reads FamilySearch images; the addendum says AI use with FamilySearch data needs separate written permission.
- Per-collection image visibility for the Brazilian and Portuguese collections as seen by a normal signed-in user.
